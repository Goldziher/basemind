Mirelle-1 thermal control

The thermal design keeps every unit of the spacecraft within its limits through a mix of surface finishes, insulation and a small number of heaters. It is deliberately simple: there are no louvres, heat pipes or pumped loops on board.

Passive control

Radiator faces carry white silicone paint & the remaining structure is black anodised to spread heat evenly. A multi-layer insulation (MLI) blanket of fifteen layers wraps the battery pack and the barrel of the imager optics, isolating them from the rapid swings of the outer panels as the spacecraft enters and leaves eclipse.

Heaters

Two Kapton film heaters are fitted. THERM-HTR-01 sits on the battery and is driven by the power system's own thermostat. THERM-HTR-02 sits on the imager focal plane and is switched by the flight software: on when the detector reads < -10 °C and off again once it reaches -5 °C. Each heater draws 1.2 W.

Analysis cases

The hot case assumes a beta angle of 70 degrees, end-of-life surface properties and continuous downlink. The cold case assumes the longest eclipse, beginning-of-life surfaces and safe mode. In both cases every unit stays within its operating range with a margin > 5 °C.
